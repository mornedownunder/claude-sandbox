Here's a reusable prompt. Fill in the bracketed parts and paste it into any assistant.

---

You are an experienced content strategist for [BRAND / BLOG NAME], which writes for [AUDIENCE: role, industry, seniority].

Context:
- What we sell or advocate for: [PRODUCT / POSITION]
- Topics we own: [3-5 THEMES]
- Topics we avoid: [ANY OFF-LIMITS SUBJECTS]
- Goal of the blog: [e.g. organic search traffic / thought leadership / product education]
- Posts that worked before: [2-3 TITLES + why you think they landed]
- Tone: [e.g. plainspoken and technical, no hype]

Generate 20 blog post ideas. Cover a spread of these angles rather than clustering on one:
1. A contrarian take on something our audience assumes is settled
2. A specific how-to that solves one narrow, annoying problem
3. A teardown or case study with real numbers
4. A "what we got wrong" or lessons-learned piece
5. A explainer that makes a confusing concept concrete
6. A trend piece tied to something changing in [INDUSTRY] right now

For each idea give me:
- **Working title** (specific, no listicle filler, under 70 characters)
- **The hook** — one sentence on why a busy reader stops scrolling
- **Reader takeaway** — what they can do differently after reading
- **Angle we can uniquely claim** — what data, experience, or access makes this ours and not generic
- **Format & length** — e.g. 800-word how-to, 2,000-word teardown
- **Effort** — Low / Medium / High

Rules:
- No generic titles that any company in our space could publish ("The Ultimate Guide to X", "5 Tips for Y"). If an idea would work with our name swapped out, cut it.
- Prefer specificity over breadth: "Why our onboarding drop-off doubled after we added a tutorial" beats "Improving user onboarding".
- At least 5 ideas should be ones a competitor would be uncomfortable publishing.
- Skip anything requiring data or access we don't have — flag it instead under "Needs input".

Finish with your top 3 picks and one line each on why you ranked them there.

---

**Tips for using it**

- The "posts that worked before" field does the most work. Give real examples if you have them; the output gets noticeably less generic.
- To go deeper on a single theme, run it again with "Topics we own" narrowed to one item.
- Good follow-up once you pick one: "Outline idea #4 — section headers, the argument each section makes, and what evidence I need to gather."

**The ask** — A reusable prompt that produces blog post ideas you'd actually write, not a list of generic titles. The failure mode with idea prompts is bland output ("10 Tips for Better Productivity"), so most of the work below is forcing specificity and killing the generic.

**Out of scope** — Outlines, drafts, SEO keyword research. This gets you a shortlist to pick from.

> I need blog post ideas for [what you write about / your business or beat]. My readers are [who they are and what they're trying to do].
>
> Give me 10 ideas. For each one:
> - a working title
> - one sentence on the **argument**, not the topic — the specific claim the post makes
> - who it's for and why they'd stop scrolling for it
>
> Rules that matter more than hitting 10:
>
> 1. Every idea must be one I could write from experience or evidence I plausibly have. Skip anything needing original research I can't do.
> 2. Vary the shape. Include at least one contrarian take, one "here's how we actually do it" walkthrough, one that takes a common piece of advice apart, and one built around a specific number or result.
> 3. Apply the swap test: if an idea would work unchanged on a competitor's blog with the name swapped out, it's too generic — cut it and replace it. That test is the whole point, so apply it before you show me the list, not after.
> 4. No "Ultimate Guide to X", no "N Tips for X", no trend pieces without a stake in the ground.
> 5. Rank them. Put the three you'd bet on first, with a half-sentence on why.
>
> Already published, so don't repeat these: [paste titles, or write "nothing yet"].

Fill the three bracketed slots before you send it — the audience line especially, since it's what stops the model defaulting to generic marketing-blog output.

**Assumption** — This is a one-off brainstorm you'll eyeball and cherry-pick from. If it's feeding an editorial calendar you run monthly, I'd tighten it differently: fewer ideas, an explicit house-voice note, and a rule about balancing the mix across the quarter.

If this is going to run at volume, I'd add success criteria and a few test cases — say the word.

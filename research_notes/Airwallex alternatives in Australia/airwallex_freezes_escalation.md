## 1. Airwallex's regulatory status in Australia (AFSL, AUSTRAC, AFCA, ADI/FCS, client money)

### Takeaway
Airwallex Pty Ltd is an ASIC-licensed AFSL holder, most often cited as AFSL 487221. It is registered with AUSTRAC and is a member of AFCA. It is **not** an ADI (bank), so balances are **not** covered by the A$250,000 Financial Claims Scheme. Airwallex says customer balances are protected by bank guarantees from APRA-regulated banks, held on trust by an independent trustee. Older terms describe funds held on trust in segregated ADI accounts.

### Inferences
- For a sole trader, the practical point is that money in Airwallex is not a bank deposit. It does not have FCS protection. The protection that exists (guarantee or trust) is about Airwallex's insolvency, not about Airwallex choosing to hold funds. A compliance hold is a separate risk from insolvency, and the bank-guarantee structure does not shorten a hold.
- Because AFCA determinations can trigger a demand on the bank guarantee under the 2023 PDS, an AFCA determination is enforceable against funds Airwallex cannot simply withhold. This is a reason to escalate to AFCA rather than only arguing with support.

## 4. Why providers often won't explain a hold: AML/CTF obligations and tipping off (incl. 2024 reforms and 2026 commencement dates)

### Takeaway
Airwallex is an AUSTRAC reporting entity. It must monitor transactions, do ongoing customer due diligence, and report suspicious matters (SMRs). Section 123 of the AML/CTF Act makes "tipping off" a criminal offence, so firms routinely refuse to give reasons for a hold. The 2024 Amendment Act replaced s123 with a test about prejudicing an investigation, **in force from 31 March 2025**. Most other reforms, including updated CDD and the requirement for AML/CTF programs to include policies to prevent tipping off, **started 31 March 2026**. Tranche 2 sectors began on 1 July 2026. Airwallex has also been under specific AUSTRAC scrutiny since January 2026, and that may make it more cautious.

### Cited Findings
- The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Amendment Act 2024 (Cth) received Royal Assent on **10 December 2024**. It passed both Houses on 28 or 29 November 2024; sources differ — [Baker McKenzie InsightPlus](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/banking-finance_1/australia-amlctf-amendment-act-overhauls-amlctf-legislative-landscape); [AUSTRAC, "AML/CTF Amendment Bill passes Parliament"](https://www.austrac.gov.au/amlctf-amendment-bill-passes-parliament); [BDO overview](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/insights/forensic-services/overview-of-the-aml-ctf-amendment-act-2024).
- **Main reforms commenced 31 March 2026** unless deferred under transitional rules — [AUSTRAC, About the AML/CTF reforms](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/about-amlctf-reforms/about-reforms); [Home Affairs, Overview of the AML/CTF Amendment Act](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/criminal-justice/Pages/overview-of-the-amlctf-amendment-act.aspx); [Dentons, 29 Jan 2026](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/january/29/current-reporting-entities-gear-up-for-imminent-aml-ctf-reforms). Firms under that kind of review typically tighten monitoring and raise more alerts, which would plausibly mean more frequent holds and document requests through 2026. This is an inference, not a documented Airwallex policy change.

## 5. How common are complaints about Airwallex holds and support, compared with Wise and Revolut?

## 6. Escalation path: Airwallex IDR (RG 271), AFCA (eligibility, cost, timeframes, powers) and ASIC

### Takeaway
(General information, not legal advice.) The path has three steps:
1. Lodge a formal written **complaint** with Airwallex. Call it a complaint and ask for an IDR response. Airwallex must give a final IDR response within **30 calendar days** under ASIC RG 271, or send a delay notice before that date with reasons and AFCA referral rights.
2. If the response is unsatisfactory or late, lodge with **AFCA**. AFCA is free, and sole traders (under 100 employees) are eligible small businesses. The general time limit is 2 years from the IDR response (or 6 years from awareness, whichever is earlier).
3. AFCA assesses whether the terms allowed the action and whether Airwallex used them **fairly**. It rarely orders an account reopened, but it can award compensation, and its determinations bind the member.

ASIC does not resolve individual disputes, but it is the place to report systemic IDR failures. AUSTRAC is not a complaints body for customers.

### Cited Findings
- **RG 271 timeframes:**
  - For complaints received on or after 5 Oct 2021, the IDR response period for standard complaints is **30 calendar days**, down from 45 — [Allens, "Get ready for the IDR changes", Aug 2021](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2021/08/get-ready-for-the-idr-changes/); [Bright Law, ASIC final updated complaints requirements](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/asic-releases-final-updated-requirements-for-complaints-handling/); [ifa, "ASIC cuts down response times for disputes"](https://www.ifa.com.au/asic-cuts-down-response-times-for-disputes).
  - Default or hardship credit complaints have 21 days, and trustee complaints 45 days — [The Adviser](https://www.theadviser.com.au/breaking-news/40630-asic-tightens-dispute-resolution-guidance).
  - If a firm can't meet the deadline, it must send a **delay notification** before the deadline expires, with reasons and the complainant's right to go to AFCA — [Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2021/08/get-ready-for-the-idr-changes/); [Gadens](https://www.gadens.com/?p=8016).
  - Firms may close a complaint within 5 business days without a full written IDR response, unless the complainant asks for a written response (or it is a hardship complaint) — [Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2021/08/get-ready-for-the-idr-changes/).
  - These are secondary law-firm summaries from 2021, so possibly outdated, but RG 271 has not been reported as amended on these points [unverified against ASIC's current RG 271 text].
- **Airwallex's own IDR process:**
  - The help centre says the Australian target for a final response is within **30 calendar days**. "Where that isn't possible, we will notify you of the delay, the reasons for the delay and the deadline for our final response."
  - If unsatisfied, Australian customers can go to AFCA. Customers should "refer to your latest Airwallex invoice to confirm the specific Airwallex entity you are contracted with" (snippets).
  - Source: [Airwallex Help, "Share Feedback"](https://help.airwallex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4593797321743-Share-Feedback).
- **AFCA eligibility:**
  - AFCA accepts complaints from small businesses with fewer than 100 employees, "which includes: a sole trader, a partnership, an incorporated business".
  - The financial firm must be an AFCA member at the time the complaint is submitted.
  - Source: [AFCA, Complaints AFCA can deal with](https://www.afca.org.au/what-to-expect/complaints-we-consider).
  - Airwallex is a member — [Airwallex AU PDS 2023](https://assets.ctfassets.net/e6wv1zvbwa49/5fliJR8TExtbNhNUMEQGjq/e24bc015cabcd769911a53bfd8430040/_2023.08.22__Airwallex_AU_-_Product_Disclosure_Statement_-_Direct_Services__CLEAN_.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Practical step sequence** for the report writer (derived from the cited rules):
  1. **Day 0:** email Airwallex support with the subject "Formal complaint – request for IDR response under RG 271". Include the account ID, the Airwallex contracting entity (from the latest invoice), amounts held, dates, documents already supplied, and the outcome sought: release of a specified amount by a date, written reasons or the clause relied on, and an end date for any reserve. Keep the ticket number.
  2. **Day 30 (calendar):** if there is no final response and no valid delay notice, or the response is unsatisfactory, lodge at AFCA (afca.org.au, free). Attach the complaint, all correspondence, terms version, Xero ledger extracts and payer statements.
  3. At AFCA, the firm gets up to 30 more days to resolve. If it makes an unsatisfactory offer, reject it in writing so the case moves to case management (matches the anecdote).
  4. **Arguments that match AFCA's test:** the terms may permit a hold, but was it exercised fairly and proportionately given the documented, low-risk income? Was the duration reasonable? Was notice given? Reasons can be withheld under s123, but AFCA can still assess the firm's conduct.
  5. **Parallel:** report IDR non-compliance to ASIC only if Airwallex breaches the RG 271 deadlines or process.
- AFCA is unlikely to force Airwallex to keep serving the person. Its value is in getting held funds released or reserves shortened, and possibly compensation (e.g. interest or costs from the hold). That fits a strategy of leaving Airwallex and using AFCA to recover the balance.

## 7. Practical risk reduction (any provider)

### Takeaway
Holds are usually triggered by transaction-monitoring alerts and by mismatches with the customer's declared profile. The risk drops if the account's declared purpose matches real activity, source-of-funds evidence is ready, large or new inflows are flagged in advance, money does not just pass straight through, and a buffer is kept at an ADI (bank) with FCS cover. Most of these steps are inferences from AUSTRAC's CDD obligations and observed hold triggers, not provider-published rules.

### Cited Findings
- Reporting entities must continuously monitor customers. A change in a customer's ML/TF risk profile (e.g. new payers, currencies or volumes) is a trigger for checking source of funds or wealth — [AUSTRAC, Ongoing CDD (Reform)](https://www.austrac.gov.au/about-us/amlctf-reform/reforms-guidance/amlctf-program-reform/customer-due-diligence-reform/ongoing-customer-due-diligence-reform); [AUSTRAC, Source of funds and source of wealth](https://austrac.gov.au/node/819); [AUSTRAC, Reviewing and updating customers' ML/TF risk and KYC information](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/customer-due-diligence/ongoing-customer-due-diligence/reviewing-and-updating-customers-mltf-risk-and-kyc-information).
- Measures suited to a customer "may change over time as the ML/TF risk profile of the customer changes" — [AUSTRAC, Enhanced CDD program](https://www.austrac.gov.au/business/core-guidance/amlctf-programs/enhanced-customer-due-diligence-ecdd-program).
- AUSTRAC specifically criticised Airwallex's transaction monitoring and suspicious-matter processes in January 2026. That is the system that generates holds — [Willkie, 29 Jan 2026](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/austrac-appoints-external-auditor-to-review-airwellexs-aml-ctf-compliance/).
- During reviews Airwallex typically requests company documents, proof of business activity, invoices and source-of-funds information — [summary of Statrys and Mobile Transaction reviews](https://www.mobiletransaction.org/airwallex-review/); [Statrys](https://statrys.com/blog/open-airwallex-account) (third-party, 2026).
- Airwallex balances are not FCS-protected, whereas ADI deposits are covered up to A$250k per account holder per ADI — [Airwallex, Is Airwallex safe?](https://www.airwallex.com/en-au/blog/is-airwallex-safe); [Finder](https://www.finder.com.au/business-banking/airwallex-global-account).

### Inferences
These are practical steps derived from the above. Present them as general guidance, not provider rules.
- **Keep the declared business profile accurate.** List every income type: short-term rental host payouts (Airbnb), self-publishing royalties (Amazon KDP and other book platforms), music distribution royalties, and traditional publisher advances or royalties. Give expected currencies, typical and maximum monthly amounts, and payout frequency. Update the profile *before* adding a new income stream.
- **Tell the provider in advance** (in writing, via a support ticket) about expected unusual credits, such as a publisher advance, a large royalty catch-up, a peak-season Airbnb payout, or a new distributor. Name the payer and give the approximate amount and date. Keep the ticket as evidence.
- **Have a source-of-funds pack ready:** platform payout statements (Airbnb earnings reports, KDP royalty reports, distributor statements), publishing contracts and royalty statements, ABN details, recent tax returns or BAS, and Xero reports reconciling each Airwallex receipt to a payer statement. Xero's bank-feed reconciliation can produce this quickly.
- **Avoid pass-through patterns.** Receiving funds and moving them straight out (especially to personal accounts, crypto or third parties) in the same amount resembles layering. Leave funds for a reasonable period, convert or pay out in line with normal business needs, and avoid many round-number transfers.
- **Use consistent names and accounts.** Payout destinations on each platform should be in the account holder's or ABN's name and match KYC. Avoid receiving third-party money unrelated to the declared business.
- **Keep a buffer in an ADI-held account** (an FCS-covered bank account) with at least 1–3 months of expenses and tax obligations. Where possible, route at least one income stream (e.g. AUD Airbnb payouts) directly to the bank, so a non-bank hold never blocks everything at once.
- **Diversify receiving rails:** keep a second provider, or a bank's multi-currency or foreign-currency account, set up and verified in advance for foreign-currency royalties. Then a hold at one provider can be routed around by changing payout details on the platforms.
- **Respond to RFIs fast and completely,** and ask in writing for (a) the specific documents needed, (b) the expected review timeframe, and (c) the clause relied on for any reserve. Escalate to a formal IDR complaint if the timeframe passes.

## Wise Business (Australia): eligibility, cost, receiving details, FX fees, Xero, regulation, holds and support

### Takeaway
Sole traders can open Wise Business in Australia. It costs a one-off A$65 to unlock local account details, with no monthly fee. It gives local receiving details in AUD, USD, GBP and EUR, plus several more currencies. Domestic-network receipts are free; USD wire/SWIFT, GBP SWIFT and EUR SWIFT receipts carry a small fixed fee. Wise integrates with Xero through bank feeds. Its regulatory status needs care: Wise Australia holds AFSL 513764 **and** a *limited* APRA ADI licence restricted to purchased payment facilities (PPF). Despite that ADI label, its own documents say balances are **not deposits and are not covered by the Financial Claims Scheme**. Its Trustpilot score is high, but its ProductReview.com.au score is very low (1.7/5), and account deactivations or frozen balances are a recurring complaint theme.

I could not open Wise's own business pricing page (wise.com/au/pricing/business) to confirm the A$65 figure there.

It is waived for card transactions, SWIFT transfers, Wise-to-Wise transfers, batch payments, and accounts holding more than a qualifying balance — [Wise Help, Cross-border fees on same-currency transfers for your business](https://wise.com/help/articles/79jNnUdgbD4IbJiUbExpqt/cross-border-fees-on-same-currency-transfers-for-your-business) (seen 15 Nov 2026). **Regulatory status in Australia**
- Wise Australia Pty Ltd holds **AFSL 513764** (ASIC). Its Combined FSG/PDS, dated **9 Feb 2026**, says it also holds a **limited ADI licence from APRA as a provider of purchased payment facilities** — [Wise AU FSG/PDS](https://wise.com/australia/fsg_pds).
- APRA granted TransferWise Australia (now Wise Australia) a limited ADI licence under the Banking Act on **30 Nov 2020** — [APRA media release](https://apra.gov.au/node/2048). A 2023 Commonwealth Gazette notice restricts its banking business to PPF. It also requires Tier 1 capital of at least the greater of $3m or 5% of stored-value liabilities, and bars paying interest on customer funds — [Conditions on banking authority 2023, Wise Australia Pty Ltd](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2023G00731/asmade/2023-07-03/text/original/pdf).
- **FCS does not apply.** "The money you have with us is not a deposit and hence the Australian Financial Claims Scheme does not apply" — [Wise AU Card Supplement agreement](https://wise.com/au/card-supplement-agreement). Funds held with Wise Australia Pty Ltd and Wise Australia Investments Pty Ltd are "not subject to the Financial Claims Scheme (FCS)" — [Wise AU Assets Customer Agreement](https://wise.com/au/legal/assets-customer-agreement).
- Wise says customer funds are "safeguarded in separate accounts with trusted financial institutions" — [Wise AU Business](https://wise.com/au/business).
- Banking Day reported (undated) that Wise is one of only two PPF providers, the other being PayPal Australia, and that a full banking licence was not on Wise's roadmap — [Banking Day](https://bankingday.com/wise-up-to-fastest-payments) (date unknown; possibly outdated).

**Holds and freeze track record** (see also the review section below)
- ProductReview.com.au: **1.7/5 from 750 reviews**, with 20% positive and 80% negative sentiment — [ProductReview.com.au, Wise (TransferWise) listing](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/transferwise) (seen 15 Nov 2026 via search snippet; index date unknown).
- Trustpilot: TrustScore about **4.3 (header shows 4.5) from 291,310 reviews** — [Trustpilot, Wise](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.wise.com) (seen 15 Nov 2026 via search snippet; header and displayed score inconsistent). Recurring complaint themes include balances held after verification requests and accounts blocked or closed without reasons (anecdotes) — same page and [au.trustpilot.com Wise](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/wise.com?page=5).
- Capterra: about 3.8/5 from about 85–91 reviews. One reviewer says Wise refused to move a balance from a suspended profile to a new business profile (anecdote) — [Capterra, Wise](https://www.capterra.com/p/234269/Wise/reviews/).
- Anecdote: on a third-party complaints site, a Sydney user whose Wise account was shut and funds frozen says Wise released the full balance after an escalation to AFCA — [Ajust, Wise complaints](https://www.ajust.com.au/complaints/wise).

### Inferences
- Wise is the most direct "like-for-like" replacement for Airwallex's receiving function: local USD, GBP and EUR details, a low one-off cost and a Xero feed. Structurally, though, it is the **same kind of entity** as Airwallex: a payment-facility provider whose balances are not FCS-protected deposits. It therefore does not remove the risk the user is leaving Airwallex over, a compliance-driven hold on their own funds.
- The 1.7/5 ProductReview score against the ~4.3 Trustpilot score suggests that Australian reviewers who write on ProductReview are disproportionately people with problems, such as closures and holds. Neither score measures how often holds actually happen.
- A practical mitigation, if Wise is used at all, is to treat it as a pass-through receiving rail and sweep balances regularly to an ADI bank account. That limits how much money a hold can trap at any one time.

- I could not confirm whether Wise has an Australian Xero App Store listing or a direct Xero feed for AU organisations; the evidence is the generic Wise help article plus UK listings.
- I found no official Wise statistics or policy page on how long verification holds typically last for Australian business accounts.
- I found no Australian news coverage specifically about Wise freezing accounts.

## Traditional banks: CBA, NAB, ANZ, Westpac, Macquarie (plus Bendigo and BOQ)

### Takeaway
All four majors offer a **$0-monthly-fee business transaction account a sole trader can open**, with a direct Xero feed on the AUD account. They are full ADIs, so deposits are FCS-protected up to $250k per account holder per ADI. Foreign-currency **accounts** are the weak point. CBA and NAB offer FCAs with no monthly fee, but charge roughly A$11–15 per inbound international payment, and opening may require a branch visit. ANZ Australia appears to offer FCAs mainly to corporate clients. Westpac has an FCA but no Xero feed for it. Macquarie's business account accepts AUD only. Only CBA had any evidence of a Xero feed for a foreign-currency account. Bendigo and BOQ both offer business FCAs.

### Cited Findings
**Financial Claims Scheme (applies to all banks below)**
- The FCS is "a government-backed safety net for deposits of up to $250,000 per account holder per ADI" — [fcs.gov.au](https://fcs.gov.au) (seen 15 Nov 2026 via snippet).
- All deposits one account holder holds with a single banking institution, including banking businesses it operates under other names, are added together before the $250,000 limit is applied. Account holders can be individuals or entities — [APRA FAQ, accounts and account holders](https://apra.gov.au/frequently-asked-questions-about-accounts-and-account-holders).

**CBA (Commonwealth Bank) — ADI**

Sole traders can open an account online — [CommBank Business Transaction Account](https://www.commbank.com.au/business/bank-accounts/business-transaction-account.html?ei=BTA2) (snippet).

### Inferences
- For this user, the banks' strength is **custody and recourse, not FX convenience**. A $0 business transaction account at a major bank, with a direct Xero feed and FCS protection, is the natural "home" account for AUD income such as Airbnb payouts and AUD-paid royalties, and for swept balances.
- Receiving USD, GBP and EUR into a big-bank FCA costs roughly A$11–15 per inbound payment, and possibly intermediary deductions. For frequent small royalty payments that is expensive compared with Wise's free local-rail receipts. It is acceptable for infrequent large payments, such as a traditional publisher's advances or royalty statements.
- CBA looks like the strongest big-bank fit for foreign-currency receiving plus Xero: it has an FCA for business, and the only (unconfirmed) evidence of a Xero feed for an FCA. Westpac's FCA has no Xero feed, Macquarie has no FCA, and ANZ appears to reserve FCAs for corporate clients.
- Note that a bank can also freeze or close an account. Holding money in an ADI protects against the institution failing (FCS), not against an AML/CTF hold or debanking. The difference is the depth of complaint handling, branch and phone access, and AFCA oversight (see the ADI section).

- I could not confirm whether CBA and NAB FCAs can now be opened online, or still need a branch.
- I could not confirm whether FCS protection extends to foreign-currency deposits at ADIs, or how it would be paid out.
- I found no ProductReview or Trustpilot scores for the banks' business products specifically.
- Customer-owned banks: I found no customer-owned bank with a business FCA that stood out.

## Payoneer for Australian sole traders

### Takeaway
Payoneer Australia Pty Ltd is an ASIC-licensed issuer of non-cash payment facilities (AFSL 504803), **not a bank**, so balances are not FCS-protected. It offers USD, EUR and GBP receiving accounts and a Xero bank-feed integration. Fees are opaque and hard to verify: an annual fee of US$29.95 below an activity threshold, and AUD withdrawal costs reported anywhere from US$1.50 flat to 1–4%. Its review reputation on frozen funds and support is poor: Trustpilot has suppressed its rating for a guideline breach, and G2 shows 3.2/5. That makes it a weak choice for someone leaving Airwallex over holds.

### Cited Findings
- Regulation: "authorised and regulated by the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)", AFSL **504803**, entity ACN **621 926 501**, issuing "non-cash payment facilities in the form of the Payoneer Payment Service" — [Payoneer Target Market Determination](https://www.payoneer.com/legal/target-market-determination/); also [Payoneer PDS](https://www.payoneer.com/vi/legal/product-disclosure-statement/) (provider documents; ASIC register not checked directly).
- Annual fee: third-party pages report **US$29.95 a year** if receipts fall below a threshold over 12 months. The threshold is given as US$2,000 by some sources and US$6,000 by others — e.g. [Finder, Payoneer review](https://www.finder.com.au/payoneer-review), [Wise AU, Payoneer vs Wise (competitor)](https://wise.com/au/blog/payoneer-vs-wise-australia) **[unverified — conflicting; Payoneer's pricing page could not be opened]**.

## ADI (bank) vs non-bank payment provider: what each option is, and why it matters for frozen funds and recourse

### Takeaway
The big four, Macquarie, Bendigo and BOQ are full ADIs: deposits are FCS-guaranteed up to $250k per account holder per ADI. Wise is technically a *limited* ADI, restricted to purchased payment facilities, but says its balances are not deposits and are **not FCS-covered**. Payoneer, OFX and Zeller are non-bank providers holding ASIC licences or arrangements. The FCS protects against a bank *failing*, not against a compliance *freeze*. Recourse for both types runs through internal disputes and then AFCA. A 2026 payments reform proposes replacing the PPF regime with "stored value facility" licensing under ASIC, with APRA supervising major providers.

### Cited Findings
- FCS covers deposits up to $250,000 per account holder per ADI — [fcs.gov.au](https://fcs.gov.au). Aggregation applies across all brands of one ADI — [APRA FAQ](https://apra.gov.au/frequently-asked-questions-about-accounts-and-account-holders).
- Wise: a limited ADI (PPF only) plus AFSL 513764, and the FCS "does not apply" — [Wise AU FSG/PDS](https://wise.com/australia/fsg_pds); [APRA 2020 release](https://apra.gov.au/node/2048); [Wise AU Card Supplement](https://wise.com/au/card-supplement-agreement).
- Payoneer: AFSL 504803, non-cash payment facility issuer — [Payoneer TMD](https://www.payoneer.com/legal/target-market-determination/).
- OFX: "not a bank account" — [OFX Help](https://www.ofx.com/en-au/business/help/).
- Recourse: Wise directs Australian complaints to AFCA after internal handling (15 days, extendable to 30) — [Wise Help, complaints](https://wise.com/help/articles/2235393/how-do-i-make-a-complaint).
- Limits of AFCA recourse on closures: a consumer forum post quotes an AFCA fact sheet saying AFCA "generally accepts that a financial firm can stop providing a financial product or service, so it is unlikely we will require the financial firm to reopen the account". The post concerns banks — [Australian Frequent Flyer forum, Debanking](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/forced-closure-of-account-debanking.123421/post-3013528) (secondary quotation; AFCA original not opened) **[unverified]**.
- Payments reform: the Tranche 1 exposure drafts were released in **March 2026**: the Treasury Laws Amendment Bill 2026 (Payments System Modernisation) and the Payment Entities (Prudential Regulation) Bill 2026. They would replace the PPF concept with "stored value facilities" under an AFS licensing model. APRA would supervise "major" SVF providers holding **$200 million or more** in stored value, aggregated across the group. Consultation closed 9 April 2026, and introduction to Parliament was targeted for 2026 — [Gilbert + Tobin, Tranche 1 at a glance](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/payments-system-modernisation-tranche-1-key-proposals-at-a-glance); [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/major-overhaul-of-australias-payment-system-regulation-key-changes-and-timeline/). Payment-safeguarding rules were deferred to a later tranche — same sources.

**Status summary** (all sourced above):

| Option | Type | FCS on balances? | Licence / authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| CBA, NAB, ANZ, Westpac, Macquarie, Bendigo, BOQ | Full ADI (bank) | Yes, up to $250k per account holder per ADI | APRA-authorised ADI |
| Wise Australia | Limited ADI (PPF only) + AFSL | **No** (Wise's own documents) | APRA limited ADI; AFSL 513764 |
| Payoneer Australia | Non-bank payment provider | No (not a deposit-taker) | AFSL 504803 |
| OFX Global Business Account | Non-bank ("not a bank account") | No | ASIC-licensed [AFSL number not verified] |
| Zeller | Non-bank | No | [not verified] |

### Inferences
- **For frozen-funds risk, the FCS is not the main issue.** It pays out if an ADI fails; it does nothing about a hold. Any AUSTRAC-regulated entity, bank or fintech, can hold funds during AML/CTF reviews. The practical differences are: (a) where the money sits if the provider fails (an FCS-guaranteed deposit, or safeguarded client money with no government guarantee); (b) access to humans, such as branches, relationship staff and phone; and (c) the scale of the provider's complaints function. All of these providers are subject to AFCA.
- Because the user's income is partly marketplace and platform payouts (Airbnb, KDP, music distributors), the platforms' own payout currencies matter. KDP pays AUD to Australian accounts for Amazon.com.au sales and USD to USD accounts for Amazon.com — [KDP Help](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/GJD7DFFX6X88AK46). Where Amazon has to convert, it uses its own rate, which includes its fees (same source). A low-risk architecture is therefore an ADI AUD account as the main account, with a non-bank FX rail used only as a pass-through.
- If the SVF reform is enacted, Wise (and possibly others above the $200m threshold) would move from the PPF/limited-ADI regime to SVF licensing with APRA prudential oversight. Wise's "ADI" label may then disappear, but the absence of FCS cover would not change.

- I could not confirm whether the payments reform bills had been introduced to or passed by Parliament as of November 2026.
- I could not open AFCA's own fact sheet on account closures.

# Revolut Business as a business banking option for an Australian sole trader (as of 15 November 2026)

Method note for the report writer: WebSearch was the only working research tool. WebFetch could not reach help.revolut.com or savings.com.au (both blocked by the sandbox network), and the Perplexity API returned a network error. So almost every finding below comes from search-result excerpts, not full-page reads. Revolut's help centre, terms and pricing pages, and the ASIC, APRA, AFCA and AUSTRAC registers, were **not** read directly. Treat specific numbers as "reported in a search excerpt of the cited page" and re-check them before a final decision. Many third-party sources are written by competitors (Airwallex, OFX, Wise, Aspire, WorldFirst), which is a bias, and it matters most for the Airwallex blog because the person is leaving Airwallex.

## 1. Is Revolut Business offered in Australia, since when, and can sole traders open accounts?

### Takeaway
Yes. Revolut Business launched in Australia on 17 May 2023, and sole traders with an ABN have been eligible since about May 2024. There is one unresolved contradiction: Revolut's own help centre reportedly lists sole traders as both supported and unsupported.

### Cited Findings
- Revolut Business went live in Australia in May 2023. Revolut's blog announced it, and trade press covered it on 24 May 2023. Australia was described as the 40th country for the business platform. — [Revolut blog: "Revolut Business is now live in Australia"](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/business/blog/post/revolut-business-is-live-in-australia/); [Accountants Daily, May 2023](https://accountantsdaily.com.au/technology/18578-revolut-launches-business-service-in-australia) *(over 12 months old: launch-era facts only)*
- At launch (2023), coverage reported two plans priced at $25 and $100 with a three-month trial, and an FX cap of A$75,000 a month. **Outdated: superseded by current pricing in Q3.** — [Accountants Daily, May 2023](https://accountantsdaily.com.au/technology/18578-revolut-launches-business-service-in-australia); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/revolut-launches-offering-seeks-banking-license-australia)
- Revolut opened Revolut Business to Australian sole traders in May 2024, saying it had already registered more than 4,000 sole traders. It claimed the move expanded its addressable Australian market by about 60%, because sole traders make up about a third of the business economy. A launch promotion (one month free on Grow or Scale) ran until 30 June 2024. — [Revolut blog: AU Business sole trader launch](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/business/blog/post/au-business-sole-trader-launch/); [Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/technology/19975-revolut-expands-services-to-australian-sole-traders); [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/fintech/news/revolut-expands-business-services-to-sole-traders-in-australia) *(May 2024, over 12 months old)*
- Revolut's Australian Business Terms refer to sole traders who hold an ABN. — [Revolut AU Business Terms](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/legal/business-terms/) (from a search excerpt; page not fetched)
- **Conflict:** a search excerpt of Revolut's AU help-centre page on supported entities lists "Sole trader" as supported but also lists "Freelancer or soletrader" as unsupported. [unverified: the page could not be fetched to read the exact wording, which may separate unregistered freelancers from ABN-registered sole traders] — [Revolut Help: Legal entities for a Revolut Business account](https://help.revolut.com/en-AU/business/help/setting-up-an-account/is-my-business-eligible/what-types-of-business-entities-are-supported/)
- On 4 February 2026, Revolut Business launched a merchant acquiring (card acceptance) product in Australia. Revolut said Australian Business monthly transaction volume had grown 235% over the previous 12 months. — [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/revolut-business-launches-merchant-acquiring-suite-in-australia); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3454410)
- Revolut reports more than one million (one outlet says 1.2 million) Australian retail customers and "thousands" of business clients. — [Revolut newsroom: Revolut Bank Australia launch](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/news/revolut_accelerates_global_banking_expansion_with_landmark_apac_licence_launches_revolut_bank_australia/); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/payments/revolut-has-12-million-australians-mortgages-could-be-next/)

### Inferences
- An Australian sole trader with an ABN appears eligible. The person should confirm in the onboarding flow that their business type (short-term rental host plus royalty and creative income) is not on the restricted or unsupported list. That list could not be read.
- With "thousands" of Australian business clients against more than a million retail customers, Revolut Business AU is a small book. That may mean less local support depth, but this is speculative.

### Gaps
- The exact supported and unsupported entity wording on the AU help page, and any restricted-industry list (for example, whether short-term accommodation or royalty income from platforms triggers enhanced due diligence), could not be read.
- No source said whether the July 2026 ADI transition changed eligibility for sole traders.

## 2. Regulatory status in Australia: AFSL, ADI, AUSTRAC, AFCA, FCS and safeguarding

### Takeaway
Since 21 July 2026, Revolut Payments Australia Pty Ltd (the entity that issues Revolut Business AU) has been an APRA-authorised ADI trading as Revolut Bank Australia. It also holds AFSL and Australian Credit Licence 517589. Eligible AUD deposits are covered by the Financial Claims Scheme, but foreign-currency balances are not. Revolut's own wording does not settle beyond doubt whether business balances are covered.

### Cited Findings
- APRA granted Revolut Payments Australia Pty Ltd a licence to operate as an authorised deposit-taking institution under the Banking Act 1959. It also authorised Revolut Australia NOHC Pty Ltd as a non-operating holding company. — [APRA media release: APRA grants ADI licence to Revolut](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/apra-grants-adi-licence-revolut) (opening of the release seen in a search excerpt only)
- The grant date is reported as 21 July 2026. The licence is described as unrestricted (no restricted-ADI phase), and Revolut is described as the first global fintech to get this kind of licence in Australia. Revolut committed nearly A$400 million to the Australian market over five years. — [Broker News](https://www.brokernews.com.au/news/breaking-news/revolut-secures-adi-licence-launches-as-a-bank-in-australia-289687.aspx); [Startup Fortune, 21 Jul 2026](https://startupfortune.com/revolut-secures-a-full-banking-licence-from-australias-apra/); [Retail Banker International](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/news/revolut-licensed-australian-bank/); [FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/payments/australian-regulator-grants-adi-licence-to-revolut/)
- Revolut's announcement: "If you're already a Revolut customer in Australia, you've been transitioned to Revolut Bank Australia, and your eligible accounts and features now benefit from ADI protections." It has a "For our Business customers" section. Existing customers were migrated automatically. — [Revolut blog: "It's official — Revolut is a bank in Australia"](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/blog/post/australia-bank-launch/); [Revolut newsroom](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/news/revolut_accelerates_global_banking_expansion_with_landmark_apac_licence_launches_revolut_bank_australia/)
- FCS coverage: eligible deposits are protected up to A$250,000 per account holder. Revolut's AU **business** help page reportedly says "up to AUD 250,000 per eligible entity". — [Revolut Business Help: How is my money protected?](https://help.revolut.com/en-AU/business/help/more/security-and-personal-data/is-my-money-protected/) (search excerpt; page not fetched); [Revolut newsroom](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/news/revolut_accelerates_global_banking_expansion_with_landmark_apac_licence_launches_revolut_bank_australia/)

- **Conflict on business FCS cover:** Revolut's older Business Product Disclosure Statement (2023) said the Business Account "is not a bank account and does not have the benefit of the Financial Claims Scheme". That predates the ADI licence and is probably superseded. A search excerpt of an unnamed third-party guide also claimed business deposits lack FCS protection. [unverified, source not isolatable] — [Revolut Business PDS v1.1.0 (2023, outdated)](https://cdn.revolut.com/terms_and_conditions/pdf/payments_australia_pty_ltd_business_product_disclosure_statement_1.1.0_1688361197_en.pdf). Against that, the current AU Business Terms reportedly state "We are an authorised deposit-taking institution (ADI)". — [Revolut AU Business Terms](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/legal/business-terms/) (search excerpt)

### Inferences
- APRA licensed the same entity that issues Business accounts (Revolut Payments Australia Pty Ltd), and the current Business Terms say "We are an ADI". So the claim that only personal accounts are bank-protected is probably out of date. Still, the person should get written confirmation from Revolut that AUD business balances are "protected accounts" under the FCS.
- [unverified, general FCS rule not sourced here] Under FCS rules a sole trader is not a separate legal entity, so a sole trader's business deposits would likely be added to their personal deposits at the same ADI under one A$250,000 cap. Confirm at fcs.gov.au.
- The person receives royalties in foreign currencies. Any USD, GBP or EUR balance held at Revolut would have **no** FCS cover. Only the AUD balance would.

### Gaps
- Could not read APRA's register of ADIs, the ASIC Professional Register, the AFCA member search or the AUSTRAC register directly (WebFetch was blocked). So the AFCA membership number and current AUSTRAC enrolment are confirmed only through Revolut's own documents.
- No current (post-July 2026) Australian Business PDS or Target Market Determination was found.

## 4. Xero integration for Australian Revolut Business accounts

### Takeaway
Yes. Revolut's help centre says there is a native Revolut-to-Xero bank feed for Australian Revolut Business accounts, included from the Basic plan. It syncs in batches about every 4–6 hours, not in real time.

### Cited Findings
- The Xero bank feed integration is available in the UK, EEA, US, Singapore and Australia. — [Revolut Help (en-US): In which countries is this integration available?](https://help.revolut.com/en-US/business/help/more/integrating-with-other-accounting-platforms/using-the-xero-bank-feed-integration/bank-feed/question-in-which-countries-is-this-integration-available/) (search excerpt)
- Bank-feed syncing is "included on our Basic plan and above". — [Revolut Xero integration page](https://www.revolut.com/business/integrations/xero-integration/)
- Setup is done in the Revolut Business portal (Integrations, then Xero, then Connect next to Bank feed). The connecting user must have completed identity checks and hold the Manage Integrations permission. Mapping to an existing Xero bank account also syncs past transactions, while a new account syncs future ones only. — [Revolut Help AU: How do I connect Revolut Business to Xero?](https://help.revolut.com/en-AU/business/help/more/integrating-with-other-accounting-platforms/using-the-xero-bank-feed-integration/bank-feed/question-how-do-i-connect-revolut-business-to-xero/)
- Revolut exports completed transactions to Xero about every 4–6 hours. — [Revolut Help AU: How does the Xero integration work?](https://help.revolut.com/en-AU/business/help/more/integrating-with-other-accounting-platforms/using-the-xero-bank-feed-integration/bank-feed/question-how-does-the-xero-integration-work/); also stated in the [Airwallex review](https://www.airwallex.com/en-au/blog/revolut-business-account-review)
- There are separate Xero integrations for expenses and bills (exporting approved expenses and paid bills, and importing Xero bills as draft payments). — [Revolut Help AU: Xero expenses](https://help.revolut.com/en-AU/business/help/managing-my-business/expenses/integration-with-xero/how-does-the-xero-integration-for-expenses-work/); [Revolut Help AU: Xero bills](https://help.revolut.com/en-AU/business/help/more/integrating-with-other-accounting-platforms/using-the-xero-bank-feed-integration/bills/when-are-bills-synchronised-xero-bills/)

### Inferences
- The feed is an API integration run by Revolut, not necessarily a Xero "direct bank feed" of the kind the big four banks provide. In practice it gives automatic transactions. Whether each foreign-currency balance syncs as its own Xero bank account was not confirmed.

### Gaps
- Whether foreign-currency sub-accounts (for example EUR) sync to separate Xero currency accounts on the AU product. This was not confirmed and matters for multi-currency reconciliation.

## 5. Track record on frozen accounts, compliance holds and closures

### Takeaway
Freezes and compliance holds are the most common complaint about Revolut on every review platform found. Revolut's terms allow blocks for review. In the 2025 UK ombudsman decisions found, most block and closure complaints were not upheld, including a business account blocked for about three months. Regulators have penalised Revolut for weak AML transaction monitoring, which is the kind of finding that tends to push a firm toward more aggressive holds. No Australia-specific freeze data or mainstream Australian coverage was found.

No actual money laundering was found. The fine was about 0.38% of Revolut Holdings Europe UAB's 2023 revenue, against a maximum of 10%. It was reported as the largest such fine the regulator has issued. *(Apr 2025, over 12 months old)* — [Irish Times, 8 Apr 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/04/08/revolut-fined-35m-by-over-money-laundering-control-failures/); [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulatory-actions/revolut-fined-by-lithuanias-central-bank); [FinTech Global, 10 Apr 2025](https://fintech.global/2025/04/10/lithuania-imposes-largest-fine-on-revolut-for-compliance-failure/). ## 6. Support model and escalation and complaints process in Australia

### Takeaway
Support is in-app chat first, with priority support reportedly on Scale (A$150/month) and above. Dedicated account managers reportedly go only to Enterprise customers. A phone line for Australian Business customers was not confirmed. Complaints go through the app (30-day response target), then to AFCA.

### Cited Findings
- Scale and Enterprise plans reportedly include priority support, while lower plans are typically limited to in-app chat. — [OFX: Wise vs Revolut Business AU 2026 (competitor)](https://www.ofx.com/en-au/blog/wise-business-vs-revolut-business-au/); [Revolut AU startup account page](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/business/open-business-account-startup/) (search excerpt)
- Dedicated account managers are reportedly for Enterprise clients, not Scale. [unverified from a Revolut primary source] — [OFX comparison](https://www.ofx.com/en-au/blog/wise-business-vs-revolut-business-au/)
- **Conflict on phone support:** one review lists telephone support alongside in-app chat, another describes a digital-only model with no phone support. — [Capterra AU](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/1005074/revolut-for-business); [Finbold](https://finbold.com/review/revolut-business-review/); [Corporate Alliance](https://corporatealliance.com/blog/payment-solution/revolut-business-aud-account-review-worth-monthly-fee/) [unverified for AU]

Unresolved complaints can go to AFCA (free, 1800 931 678). — [Revolut AU Financial Services Guide](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/legal/financial-services-guide/); [Revolut AU Business Terms](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/legal/business-terms/)

- For a sole trader on Basic or Grow, the realistic support path during a freeze is in-app chat, then a formal complaint, then AFCA. That is structurally similar to the poor experience the person had with Airwallex. Priority support needs the A$150/month Scale plan, and even that does not include an account manager.
- AFCA is a meaningful backstop, but the 30-day internal complaint window means funds could stay inaccessible for at least a month before external escalation.

## 7. Revolut personal as an alternative: why it is unsuitable for business income

### Takeaway
Revolut's Australian personal terms bar business use, and its Business Terms say Business accounts are for commercial purposes only. Running Airbnb payouts and royalties through a personal Revolut account would breach the terms and invite exactly the closure or freeze risk the person wants to avoid.

### Cited Findings
- Revolut's Australian personal terms (Revolut Payments Australia Pty Ltd, 2023 version) say a customer may not use a personal account for business purposes, may not hold more than one personal account, and may not reopen after Revolut closes one. Revolut may refuse or stop services where use breaches these restrictions. *(2023 document, possibly superseded)* — [Revolut AU Personal Terms v0.4.1 (2023)](https://cdn.revolut.com/terms_and_conditions/pdf/payments_australia_pty_ltd_personal_terms_0.4.1_1678769253_en.pdf)
- Revolut's Business Terms (updated 16 December 2025) say Business accounts are for commercial or business purposes only, not personal use. — [Revolut Business Terms v1.4.0, 16 Dec 2025](https://cdn.revolut.com/terms_and_conditions/pdf/business_terms_cd90473c_1.4.0_1765901495_en.pdf) (search excerpt; this PDF's jurisdiction was not confirmed as Australia)
- Since 21 July 2026, Revolut personal customers in Australia sit under Revolut Bank Australia, with eligible AUD deposits FCS-covered up to A$250,000. — [Revolut blog: bank launch](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/blog/post/australia-bank-launch/)

### Inferences
- Using a personal account for ABN income would breach the terms, and that breach could itself trigger a freeze or closure. It also mixes business and personal records for Xero and ATO purposes. It is not a workaround.

### Gaps

## 8. Overall: would Revolut Business be better than Airwallex on freezes and support?

### Takeaway
Probably not, based on what could be found. Revolut Business is available to ABN sole traders, has a Xero bank feed, and (since July 2026) is an APRA-regulated ADI with FCS cover for eligible AUD deposits. But its freeze and compliance-hold record, chat-first support, and AML-monitoring history give no evidence that it would reduce the person's main risk. It may also handle USD and GBP receiving worse for an Australian account.

### Cited Findings
- Bank status and FCS cover for AUD deposits since 21 July 2026. — [APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/apra-grants-adi-licence-revolut); [Revolut](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/blog/post/australia-bank-launch/)
- Freezes are the most common complaint, and the UK ombudsman upheld Revolut's right to block a business account for about three months. — [Financer](https://financer.com/review/revolut-business/); [FOS decisions](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decisions-case-studies/ombudsman-decisions/search?BusinessName=Revolut+Ltd&Business=5721)
- AML transaction-monitoring fine (Bank of Lithuania, April 2025). — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/04/08/revolut-fined-35m-by-over-money-laundering-control-failures/)
- Local receiving details for AU Business reportedly AUD and EUR only. — [OFX](https://www.ofx.com/en-au/blog/revolut-alternatives-and-competitors-au/)

### Inferences
- Revolut's one structural improvement over a non-bank e-money or payments provider is ADI status: APRA prudential supervision and FCS cover for AUD balances. That addresses safety of funds if the provider fails, not access to funds during a compliance hold, which is the person's actual pain point.
- If the person tries Revolut anyway, a lower-risk approach (inference) is to keep a primary AUD account at a major Australian ADI with a Xero direct feed and in-branch or phone support, and treat any fintech, including Revolut, as a secondary FX or receiving account with limited balances.

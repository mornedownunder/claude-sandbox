## Overall verdict: can an Australian sole trader (ABN, no US entity) open a Brex account?

### Takeaway
No. Brex is not an option. Its own legal terms exclude this person on two separate grounds: sole proprietors are barred, and so are companies organised outside the United States. Its application rules also require a US EIN, US incorporation, US operations and a US physical address. The Capital One takeover (closed April 2026) is framed around serving "U.S. businesses" and changes none of this.

### Cited Findings
- Brex's Platform Agreement (s1.1) says individual consumers, sole proprietors, unincorporated partnerships, and companies organised or registered outside the United States may not open a Brex account. Eligible applicants are US-organised C-corps, S-corps, LLCs or LLPs with a valid US EIN. — [Brex Platform Agreement](https://www.brex.com/legal/platform-agreement) (effective date not seen)
- Brex's support page lists the application requirements: a US EIN, valid US incorporation, US operations, a US physical address, and not being in a restricted industry. Meeting them does not guarantee approval. — [Brex: Account requirements](https://www.brex.com/support/brex-account-requirements) (page date not seen)
- Capital One's closing announcement describes the deal as aiming to "deliver an integrated banking platform to U.S. businesses." — [Capital One newsroom: Capital One Completes Acquisition of Brex](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/capital-one-completes-acquisition-of-brex/)

### Inferences
- The person fails every hard gate: entity type (sole trader), country (Australia), tax ID (ABN, not EIN) and US presence. Higher pricing tiers and talking to sales cannot get around these, because they are in the legal agreement, not just in marketing.
- The only theoretical route is to form a US entity (LLC or C-corp) with US operations and a US address, then apply as that entity. For an Australian sole trader earning Airbnb payouts and royalties, this adds a foreign entity plus cross-border tax and compliance work, to reach a product built mainly around corporate cards and spend management. That is out of proportion to the need. This is a judgement, not tax advice.
- Even for eligible customers, Brex has a record of mass account closures (2022, below). That is directly relevant to someone leaving Airwallex because of blocked access to funds.

### Gaps
- I could not open the legal pages to read their effective or "last updated" dates.
- I found no official Brex page that addresses Australian applicants by name. The verdict rests on the general exclusion of non-US-organised companies.

## Brex's current eligibility rules: countries, entity types, sole proprietors, funding and revenue thresholds

### Takeaway
Brex accepts only US-organised, registered business entities (C-corp, S-corp, LLC, LLP) with a US EIN, US operations and a US address. It explicitly excludes sole proprietors, consumers, unincorporated partnerships and any company organised outside the US. It also sets cash or revenue thresholds for some products.

### Cited Findings
- **Entity types and country:** Sole proprietors, individual consumers, unincorporated partnerships and companies organised or registered outside the US may not open an account. Eligible entities are US-organised C-corps, S-corps, LLCs and LLPs with a valid US EIN. — [Brex Platform Agreement](https://www.brex.com/legal/platform-agreement)
- **Non-US companies, repeated in the account terms:** Companies organised or registered outside the US cannot use the service. The same terms allow authorised users located outside the US, meaning a US company's staff abroad. — [Brex Business Account Terms & Brex Treasury Account Agreement](https://www.brex.com/legal/bba-treasury-terms)
- **Sole proprietors:** Brex's application help page says incorporating means converting a sole proprietorship into a formally recognised, state-registered company. — [Brex support: Submitting a Brex application](https://brex.com/support/submitting-an-application)
- **Application requirements:** A US EIN, valid US incorporation, US operations and a US physical address, and not being in a restricted industry. Meeting them does not guarantee approval. — [Brex: Account requirements](https://www.brex.com/support/brex-account-requirements)
- **Thresholds (Brex's own support page, as summarised in search extracts):** These apply to the card product with monthly payments.
  - Funded startups need a minimum cash balance of $50,000 (USD), possibly lower if referred by certain partners.
  - Pre-funding startups can still apply.
  - Mid-market and enterprise companies need more than $400,000 a month in revenue.
  - "Commercial" businesses need more than $500k a year in revenue.
  - Source: [Brex: Account requirements](https://www.brex.com/support/brex-account-requirements). [unverified: I could not open the page to confirm exact wording, whether the thresholds apply only to the card, or the page date.]
- **Business account (provider claim):** Brex's marketing says its business account has no minimum balance and no monthly fees. — [Brex: The 5 Best Business Banking Accounts to Open Online in 2026](https://www.brex.com/spend-trends/business-banking/best-online-banks-and-business-banking-accounts)
- **Non-US founders:** A Brex blog says a non-US founder can apply after incorporating a US company, for example through Stripe Atlas, even while the EIN is pending. This still needs a US entity, so it is not an exception for foreign businesses. — [Brex: How to Get EIN-Only Business Credit Cards Without an SSN](https://www.brex.com/spend-trends/corporate-credit-cards/best-business-credit-cards-with-ein-only)
- **Extra restrictions:** Further limits can apply where the company or a beneficial owner or controlling officer is a public company, a broker-dealer, or certain shareholders or officers of a public company. — [Brex Business Account Agreement](https://www.brex.com/legal/product-terms/treasury/agreement)

### Inferences
- An Australian sole trader with an ABN fails the entity-type test and the country test independently. The funding and revenue thresholds never come into play.
- In the extracts, the thresholds appear tied to the card and credit product rather than to the deposit account. Either way they would be a further barrier for a small, non-venture-backed business.

### Gaps
- I could not read the effective dates of the Platform Agreement or the Business Account Terms, or the date the requirements page was last updated.
- I could not confirm whether the $50k and $500k-revenue thresholds also apply to opening the business (deposit) account alone.

## History that still matters: the 2022 small-business exit. Is it still in place in 2026?

### Takeaway
In June 2022 Brex told tens of thousands of small businesses without professional funding that their accounts would close on 15 August 2022, then admitted it had handled the communication badly. In 2026 the rule is no longer strictly "venture-backed only": the requirements page also lists pre-funding startups and "commercial" businesses over $500k in revenue. Small, non-startup businesses still face high thresholds, and the US-entity rule applies to everyone. *All 2022 sources are more than 12 months old (dated June–Nov 2022). They describe a past event, not current policy.*

### Cited Findings
- **June 2022 announcement:** Brex said it would stop serving small businesses without professional backing (VC, angel or accelerator funding). Affected accounts would stay open until 15 August 2022 so customers could move their money. The CEO said funded startups were not affected. — [TechCrunch, 17 Jun 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/17/as-brex-exits-the-smb-space-its-ceo-says-that-doesnt-include-startups-at-least-the-funded-ones/); [Axios Pro, 17 Jun 2022](https://www.axios.com/pro/fintech-deals/2022/06/17/brex-closing-smb-accounts) *(dated; >12 months old)*
- **Brex's explanation:** Brex said it was less suited to smaller customers because their needs were very different from those of tech startups. — [TechCrunch, 17 Jun 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/17/brex-which-started-out-serving-startups-now-says-it-is-less-suited-to-meet-the-needs-of-smaller-customers) *(dated)*
- **Admission:** Brex admitted it "did a poor job explaining" the decision to cut off SMBs. Some venture-backed customers who had wrongly received closure notices were later reinstated. — [TechCrunch, 23 Jun 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/23/brex-admits-it-did-a-poor-job-explaining-its-decision-to-cut-off-smbs) *(dated; the reinstatement detail comes from search extracts [unverified against article text])*
- **Reputation cost:** Co-CEO Henrique Dubugras said Brex "took a huge reputation hit" from dropping these customers. — [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/fintech-brex-reputation-hit-after-dropping-small-business-customers/636097/); [Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/brex-co-ceo-fintech-took-reputation-hit-after-dropping-customers/635976/) *(2022; dated)*
- **Later retrospective:** Dubugras gave his account of the pivots and layoffs. — [TechCrunch, 2 Nov 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/02/brex-co-founder-henrique-dubugras-details-decisions-behind-pivots-layoffs-going-remote) *(dated)*
- **2026 position (provider page, via search extract):** Funded startups need a $50k minimum cash balance, pre-funding startups can apply, mid-market and enterprise need more than $400k a month in revenue, and "commercial" businesses need more than $500k a year. — [Brex: Account requirements](https://www.brex.com/support/brex-account-requirements) [unverified wording]

### Inferences
- The 2022 policy has been replaced by a segmented system (startups, commercial, mid-market and enterprise) rather than kept as a blanket ban. Very small, non-funded businesses are still effectively outside Brex's target market.
- The episode is a concrete precedent for Brex closing a whole customer segment at short notice, about eight weeks from announcement to closure. That is relevant to someone whose main grievance with Airwallex is losing access to funds.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 primary source in which Brex formally states that the 2022 policy has been withdrawn or changed. The "evolved" reading comes from comparing the current requirements page with the 2022 coverage.

## Does Brex have a product for Australian businesses (global offering or local entities)? Does it need a US parent?

### Takeaway
Brex's "global" features (local-currency cards in 50+ countries, international subsidiaries, local reimbursements) are add-ons for companies that already hold a Brex account through a US entity. I found no Brex product that lets an Australia-only business, let alone a sole trader, open an account directly. The legal terms exclude companies organised outside the US.

### Cited Findings
- **Pricing tiers and entities:**
  - Essentials ($0) includes "up to two entities (1 global entity)" and "1 local card program".
  - Premium ($12 per user per month) adds "Multi-entity (US & International)".
  - Enterprise (custom pricing) adds unlimited US or global entities and local card issuance.
  - Source: [Brex Pricing](https://www.brex.com/pricing)
- **Local cards:** The pricing page lists local-currency cards in 50+ countries. A Brex blog says 60+ countries on Enterprise, so the figures conflict. — [Brex Pricing](https://www.brex.com/pricing); conflicting figure in Brex spend-trends blog content [unverified, exact page not confirmed]
- **Non-US accounts:** Brex support says that when you open accounts outside the United States, you are opening them with Brex's banking and payments partners. Cards are issued in the currency of the employee's entity's billing currency. — [Brex support: Accounts with multiple entities](https://www.brex.com/support/multi-entity-accounts)
- **Local reimbursements:** Locally-funded reimbursements require at least one additional legal entity on the account. — [Brex support: Locally-funded reimbursements](https://www.brex.com/support/locally-funded-reimbursements)
- **Third-party claim, lower credibility (Q&A aggregator, not Brex):** Brex accommodates US parents with US or non-US subsidiaries, and non-US parents that have at least one US legal entity. A US entity is described as a "strict requirement" for global features. — [answers.org](https://answers.org/brex/does-brex-offer-local-currency-accounts-and-international-card-issuance-for-global-startups) [unverified]
- **Third-party Australian review, lower credibility, 2026:** It calls Brex "global cards, not AU-first" and says it is not designed for Australia-only businesses. It advises Australian businesses to confirm with Brex sales whether an Australian-incorporated entity can hold an account without a US parent. — [The AI Directory AU: Brex Review Australia 2026](https://www.theaidirectory.com.au/review-brex-australia-2026/) [unverified]

### Inferences
- Combined with the Platform Agreement's exclusion of companies organised outside the US, the evidence points to Australian entities only being serviceable as additional entities under a Brex account held by a US entity. A sole trader is not an entity of the kind Brex onboards in any country.

### Gaps
- I found no official Brex page that addresses Australian companies directly, or that names its Australian banking or payments partner.
- I could not confirm whether Brex issues local AUD cards in Australia specifically. Australia is not named in the extracts I saw.
